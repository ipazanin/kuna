# Investments: every buy and sell in one place, with what it earned

How Kuna gets a third app, Investments, that keeps every trade, dividend, deposit, exchange and transfer of the owner's brokers in its own Drive file, with cash per account, values it at daily closes from free sources, shows value, gain and return in euros, and imports the owner's portfolio workbook first, from five research lanes and an independent critique checked against 210875f, with the three decisions it needs from the owner.

From five lanes: the prototype and the export, [the fit in Kuna's code](research/investments/lane2-kuna-fit.md), [the maths and Croatian tax](research/investments/lane3-maths-and-tax.md), [free price data](research/investments/lane4-price-data.md) and [the design](research/investments/lane5-design.md), with the mockup, then revised after [the critique](research/investments/critique.md). Kuna was read at `210875f` with `git show HEAD:`, because another agent is editing its working tree, so every `file:line` is against that commit. HEAD has since moved to `f35d0b0`, which changed five files. Of the cited ones only `scripts/visual-check.mjs` moved, by one line. valore was read at `6e2fd62`. Every figure below is made up, apart from public rates and thresholds. The owner's files were read for their structure only: columns and kinds of row.

The short version: Investments is a third Kuna app with its own file, `kuna-investments.json`, shaped like Loans. It stores what the brokers booked (trades, income, deposits and withdrawals, fees, currency exchanges, transfers, splits and a spin-off) and works everything else out from them, with FIFO lots, cash per account, exact decimals for quantities and prices, and euros at the ECB rate of each day. The first import is the owner's portfolio workbook, which already merges the brokers' statements into one table with 17 kinds of row. Prices arrive once a day in a second Drive file that Kuna only reads. How they get there is the first decision. valore is not the base. It tracks net worth from snapshots, and its own plan rules out a transaction ledger and any return or tax calculation (`docs/plan.md:178`, `:208`). Its care with missing prices, decimal strings and quote dates carries over, not its code.

Building waits for the Loans items in the queue (the roadmap's investments entry). It starts when the owner has reviewed this plan and answered the three decisions at the end.

  - **Value over time**, month ends, the value in blue against the dashed grey line of what you put in less what you took out, so the gap is the gain. 1Y, 3Y, 5Y, All, and a Table toggle.
  - **Where the money is**, a bar list by class (Stocks, Bonds, Money market, Cash and currencies, Crypto, Gold) or by currency.
  - **Where the gain comes from**: on what you hold, realised on sales, income, account fees and currency on cash, adding up to the total gain, with "Trading fees of €182.30 are inside these figures" under it.

Columns: holding (name over symbol, exchange and a note such as "in US dollars"), quantity, price in its own currency, cost, value, gain in € and %, weight, last day. Facts: cost, average cost, weight, last day, first bought, dividend yield. A price chart in the quoted currency with the average cost dashed and a dot per buy and a hollow one per sale. ## Settled without asking

- **The app is `investments`,** named Investments, Croatian "Ulaganja", with routes under `/investments`, the file `kuna-investments.json`, the icon `i-lucide-trending-up` and a plum identity colour, `--app-investments` #8a3f8c light and #d08ad3 dark. Lane 5 checked it against Kuna's contrast formula (`tests/tokenContrast.spec.ts:22`). Loans set the shape (`src/apps/loans/app.ts:24-68`).
- **What the brokers booked is stored, everything else is worked out.** Holdings, cost, cash, gains, income and allocation come from the records plus daily prices and rates (lane 3). valore's snapshots cannot give a gain (`docs/plan.md:178`).
- **The workbook is the first import.** The owner's `portfolio-transactions.xlsx` has a Transactions tab merged from the brokers' statements over several accounts, plus Yahoo Corrections (rows marked as real errors in Yahoo or not), Open Items and Checks tabs. It carries ISIN, executed and split-adjusted quantities and prices, commission, net cash, the counter leg of each exchange and the source file of every row. One broker can keep trading after another account opens, so accounts come from the rows, never from a cut-over date. The Yahoo export is not imported, since the workbook supersedes it and the owner's own corrections list many places where it is wrong. The roadmap already says so.
- **One record per instrument, carrying the listing it is priced by.** Gains belong to the instrument and one price source values it. A separate listing kind, as valore has (`types.ts:16-33`), would be a second record with nothing to do yet. The ISIN is optional and unique when given. Symbols are never the identity: a broker can book the same fund under one symbol one year and another the next, and Tiingo's `SEMI` is a different fund from Yahoo's `SEMI.AS` (lane 4).
- **A trade is in its instrument's quote currency.** The write rules refuse a trade in another currency, since the price and currency parts are undefined for it. Every instrument in the owner's files trades in one currency.
- **Instruments are stocks, bonds, money market, crypto or gold,** and currencies are the cash each account holds, so the allocation still shows the owner's six classes. An ETF or fund counts under what it holds, so an equity ETF is stocks and a gold ETC is gold. Kuna guesses, the owner picks.
- **FIFO, one way, everywhere.** ZPDoh Art. 70(5) requires a FIFO register (lane 3), so the app and any tax figure never disagree. "Average cost" is the cost of the open lots over their quantity. There is no method setting.
- **One FIFO queue per instrument across accounts.** Lane 3 reads the register as kept per kind of asset, not per broker. A transfer between the owner's accounts moves units or cash and closes nothing, and the lots keep their dates and costs. The workbook has share transfers between accounts, at one broker and across brokers. The owner's accountant can say otherwise (decision 3).
- **Cost includes fees everywhere.** A buy's lot costs price × quantity + fees. A sale's proceeds are price × quantity − fees.
- **One rounding rule for every split of a euro figure:** a sale's proceeds and fee across the lots it closes (by quantity), the closed and open parts of a lot, a spin-off's cost across the parent's lots. Each part but the last is rounded half away from zero to the cent and the last takes the rest, so the parts always add up. A €1.00 fee over three one-unit lots is €0.33, €0.33 and €0.34.
- **Cash per account (decision 2, recommended).** Each account holds a balance per currency, moved by every record: a buy takes q × p + fees, a sale adds q × p − fees, income adds gross − withheld, deposits, withdrawals, fees and refunds move it, an exchange takes one currency and adds the other, and a cash transfer moves it between accounts. A split, a spin-off and a share transfer move no cash. A balance below zero is allowed and shown as a warning, since a gap in the rows is the usual cause. Cash is valued at the ECB rate like any holding.
- **The headline is what the owner asked for:** total gain in euros with its %, and the money-weighted yearly return beside it. Total gain is value − what you put in less what you took out, and the % is measured on that net amount, as lane 3 has it. When more was taken out than put in, the % is hidden with a note and the yearly return carries the figure. Before the first deposit is a year old the Yearly return tile reads "Not a year yet", since an annual rate over a few weeks reads wild (lane 3).
- **Value over time is set against what you put in less what you took out,** as a step line, so the gap is the total gain at every month end.
- **Exact decimals beside Money.** `Money` stays integer cents with no currency (`src/shared/money/money.ts:5`), used by about 200 files. Quantities, unit prices, foreign amounts and rates are a new `Decimal`, a `bigint` of twelve decimal places, as valore keeps twelve (`decimal.ts:3-5`) and Loans already does exact maths in `BigInt` (`src/apps/loans/bankLoans/domain/yearlyRate.ts:49-53`). Euro figures are rounded to cents once, where they become `Money`. Stored fields keep their text (`src/shared/storage/domain/storedFields.ts:55-62`), so a decimal is read from text and never through a JavaScript number. The one exception is a spreadsheet's number cell, which arrives as a number, and the import rounds it (see Import).
- **No currency-amount type.** Every foreign figure sits on a record that names its currency once, so lane 2's `CurrencyAmount` would only repeat it.
- **Every foreign record stores its rate,** in units of the currency per euro as the ECB writes it. The rule is one: a record where euros actually moved carries those euros, and everything else carries the ECB rate of its day. So an exchange's euro leg is its euro value, a Revolut top-up in dollars carries Revolut's own rate (the workbook's `Revolut FX rate`), and a dollar trade, dividend or fee carries the ECB rate. History never moves when a rate file changes. Tax never reuses a stored rate (see Tax).
- **Holdings are valued at the ECB rate on or before the price's own date,** so a US close and its rate belong to the same day (lane 3). Euros are the only base currency.
- **Each asset kind:**
  - _Shares, ETFs and funds_ are units at a close. The owner's 29 instruments are all of this kind: 25 US listings, two on Xetra and two on Amsterdam.
  - _Money market funds_ are units at a NAV, entered by hand.
  - _Bonds held directly_ come in stage 13. Their nominal is the quantity and the clean price is in % of nominal. A lot costs nominal × price ÷ 100 + fees + the accrued interest paid, which the trade records as typed from the broker's note, since it runs to the settlement day. The next coupon pays that accrued interest back: that part lowers the lot's cost and only the rest is income. Value is nominal × price ÷ 100 plus accrued interest to the price's date. Only annual coupons, on the maturity's day and month, ACT/ACT. A treasury bill is a bond with a coupon of zero.
  - _Physical gold_ comes in stage 13, held in grams and valued at the euro gold price per troy ounce ÷ 31.1034768. A gold ETC is a listed security.
  - _Crypto_ comes in stage 13, priced in euros directly, since the ECB has no rate for it.
- **Splits are records** with `new` and `old` as whole numbers, so 1-for-3 is exact. A split multiplies the quantity of every lot bought before it by new ÷ old and leaves the cost alone (Art. 67(4)(3)). The workbook gives executed quantities and prices, so no imported trade is already adjusted.
- **Spin-offs are records.** The workbook has one. A status change without cash is not a disposal (Art. 67(4)(2)), and the first acquisition value stays (Art. 67(5)), so the new instrument gets one lot per open parent lot with the parent's date, quantity pro rata, and the record's share of the parent lot's cost, which leaves the parent lot. Without the record, the later sale of the spun-off shares would wait for a buy that never happened.
- **Prices are stored as they closed, not adjusted.** Kuna converts a past close into today's units with its split records. When the price source reports a split, Kuna offers to add the record ("NVDA split 10 for 1 on 10 Jun 2024. Add it?"). One source of truth for splits.
- **Hand-entered prices are records in the Investments file.** They cannot be fetched again, so they do not belong in a file a job rewrites (lane 4 put them there). An automatic close wins over a hand price for the same day.
- **Price history lives in its own Drive file,** `kuna-investments-prices.json`. Every save uploads the whole Investments file and Drive keeps 100 revisions forever plus 30 daily backups (`src/shared/storage/infrastructure/driveDocument.ts:33-35`), so daily closes do not belong in it. The prices file keeps every daily close from each instrument's first transaction (the first deposit for the benchmark) and the daily ECB series of each currency it needs. My estimate for the owner's 29 instruments is about 1 MB, a tenth of what `File.setContent` takes, so nothing is thinned.
- **Stale prices** follow lane 4: a listed price is late after three weekdays, a hand price after 35 days. Late prices still count, each row shows its date, and the header says how many are late. A holding never priced is named and left out, and the tile reads "Known value", as valore does (`calculations.ts:32-35`).
- **"Last day", never "Today".** Let D be the newest close date among held instruments and D′ the latest earlier date on which any of them closed. Last day is the portfolio valued at D minus the portfolio valued at D′, each holding at its latest close on or before that date, with today's quantities and cash, and each holding rounded to cents before the difference. A holding that did not trade between D′ and D adds nothing, so a bond's move over months never lands on one day.
- **A browser-kept Investments file has hand prices only.** The prices file needs Drive. Settings says so, and Kuna recommends Drive at the first run.
- **Allocation is a bar list in one hue.** Five or six class colours fail the palette check in both themes (lane 5). The portfolio is `--series-3` on every chart and anything compared with it is `--chart-deemphasis`, dashed.
- **The bar list stays in the app** as `ShareBars.vue`. A component moves to `src/shared/ui` when a second app needs it (`.agents/skills/kuna-design/SKILL.md:21`). Lane 5 proposed shared.
- **No Income screen.** The Overview tile and Transactions filtered to income carry it. Income imports from the workbook: 141 dividends with their withholding, 3 interest payments, one stock yield payment and 4 referral credits.
- **Company's import panel and `PeriodBar` stay in Company.** They are built round bank statements and Company's filters (`src/apps/company/imports/presentation/ImportPanel.vue:90-100`, `src/apps/company/filters/presentation/PeriodBar.vue:4-11`). Investments shares the workbook reading, which is already in `src/shared/xlsx/sheetGrids.ts:35`, and the row key, which moves.
- **The import asks, never invents.** A sale beyond what is held waits until the missing row arrives, and the write rules refuse a holding below zero. A row that needs a partner waits until it has one.
- **No ISIN lookup.** OpenFIGI sends no CORS header (lane 4). The workbook gives an ISIN for 19 of the 29 instruments, and the owner types the rest where one is wanted.
- **Time-weighted return splits the period at every deposit and withdrawal,** as lane 3 has it: each piece's return is the value just before the next flow ÷ the value just after the previous one, chained. With cash per account, buys and sales are not flows, so a sale and a rebuy in one month change nothing. The value on a flow day uses each holding's latest close on or before it, which the unthinned prices file gives.
- **The rate solver is shared.** XIRR and the Loans EKS estimate are the same root search over dated flows. Whichever lands first puts the bisection in `src/shared/money/rateSolver.ts`, and each passes its own time measure: days ÷ 365 for XIRR, the directive's months and days for the EKS (loans scenarios plan).
- **Not in this wave:** gifts with their own rules, coin-to-coin swaps, short positions, semi-annual coupons, yield to maturity, a dividend calendar, net worth across apps and whether it counts the company (lane 2), and an account breakdown of allocation. Each waits until the owner asks.

## Where the lanes disagreed

- **The input changed after the lanes ran.** Lanes 1, 3 and 5 read the Yahoo export. The owner then added the workbook, a Revolut trading statement CSV and IBKR activity statements. The folder also holds `yahoo-portfolio-import.csv` in Yahoo's 17 columns. Its buy and sell rows match the workbook's buys plus the spin-off and its sales, so it looks made from the workbook to load into Yahoo (unconfirmed).
- **A suffix does not give the currency.** An Amsterdam listing such as `SEMI.AS` quotes in US dollars on Yahoo (lane 1), while `VUSA.AS` trades in euros. Lanes 3 and 4 took `SEMI.AS` for a euro ETF. The suffix guess is wrong for it, which is why the import takes the currency from the rows.
- **Lane 5's "no cash ledger" and its "% on what you put in"** do not fit together. Lane 3 kept cash per account, and the workbook makes that an import rather than typing. Decision 2 recommends it.
- **US prices from Tiingo, not Alpha Vantage.** Lane 4 put Alpha Vantage first for US and Xetra. With 25 US instruments, Alpha Vantage's 25 calls a day would leave nothing for retries. Tiingo allows 1,000 a day with 30 years of history from a server (lane 4), so Alpha Vantage keeps its budget for the four European listings.
- **ECB rates come with the prices, not from the page.** Lane 2 had the page call the ECB, at the cost of a CSP host and a sentence in the privacy page. The recommended job fetches them with everything else, so the page talks to no new host.
- **Free European history may be longer than lane 4 found.** Alpha Vantage's documentation, loaded on 30 Oct, says `TIME_SERIES_MONTHLY` covers "25+ years of historical data" and does not tag it premium, and the critique's request with the demo key returned monthly IBM closes back to 1999. `outputsize=full` on the daily series is "available to premium keys". Unconfirmed until tried with a free key on a Xetra listing.

## How it is built

A new app folder, `src/apps/investments/`, with features `workbook/`, `holdings/`, `prices/`, `transactions/`, `imports/`, `performance/`, `overview/`, `home/`, `settings/`, `onboarding/` and `sync/`, each with `domain/`, `application/`, `infrastructure/` and `presentation/` as it needs them.

### Numbers

`src/shared/money/decimal.ts`, since lint allows `Intl` only in `src/shared/money`, `calendar` and `text` (`eslint.config.js:120-128`):

```ts
/** An exact decimal with up to twelve places, as a whole number of its smallest step. */
export type Decimal = bigint & { readonly __unit: 'decimal-12' }

/** Decimal text as a record stores it, such as `0.00841` or `-3.5`. Null for anything else. */
export function decimalOfText(text: string): Decimal | null
/** Typed into a field, with a decimal point or a decimal comma, as typedAmount reads money (src/shared/money/typedAmount.ts:8-11). */
export function typedDecimal(text: string): Decimal | null
export function decimalText(value: Decimal): string
export function timesDecimal(left: Decimal, right: Decimal): Decimal
/** Left × numerator ÷ denominator, rounded half away from zero to twelve places, for a split's new ÷ old. */
export function scaledDecimal(left: Decimal, numerator: bigint, denominator: bigint): Decimal
/** The amount in euros at a rate of units per euro, rounded half away from zero to the cent. */
export function inEuros(amount: Decimal, unitsPerEuro: Decimal | null): Money
```

`moneyFormat.ts` gains `formatQuantity(value, maximumDecimals)` and `formatUnitPrice(value, currency)`, beside `formatMoney` (`moneyFormat.ts:87-89`). A currency is an ISO 4217 code checked with `Intl.supportedValuesOf('currency')`, in `src/shared/money/currencyCode.ts`. BTC and XAU are not currencies here, since `Intl` writes them with two decimals (lane 2).

### Holdings, lots, cash and corporate actions

`holdings/domain/lots.ts` walks one instrument's trades, splits and spin-offs in date order (a split or spin-off before the trades of its day, buys before sells on one day, then the file's order):

- A buy opens a lot: date, quantity, cost in its currency and in euros (`inEuros(quantity × price + fees, rate)`), and the date it turns tax-free, the day after its second anniversary (lane 3 could not confirm how the anniversary itself counts).
- A sale closes the oldest lots first and gives one realised line per lot it touches: units, euro cost, euro proceeds less its share of the fee, days held, and whether it was past two years.
- A split multiplies the open quantity of every lot before it by new ÷ old.
- A spin-off opens, in the received instrument, one lot per open parent lot with the parent's date and its share of cost, and takes that cost off the parent lot.
- The result is the open lots, the realised lines, and refusals for a sale or a unit transfer beyond what its account holds on its day. A refused sale moves no cash either.

`holdings/domain/accountCash.ts` sums each account's balance per currency from every record's cash effect, and gives each foreign balance its euro amount in: the sum of its movements in euros at their own rates. `holdings/domain/holding.ts` adds the price, the rate and income: value, cost of open lots, unrealised gain, realised gain, income, the price and currency parts, average cost, weight and last day. Each account's units of an instrument come from its trades and transfers, while the lots stay one queue per instrument.

### The figures

`holdings/domain/portfolioFigures.ts` and `performance/domain/`. Two sign conventions, each named once in code: for XIRR a deposit is negative and a withdrawal and today's value are positive, the investor's view. For the time-weighted return a flow is positive when money enters the portfolio.

| Figure                          | How                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                    |
| ------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Value                           | Σ quantity × last close ÷ rate on the close's day, plus every cash balance ÷ today's rate, rounded per holding and per balance. Holdings with no price left out and named                                                                                                                                                                                                                                              |
| Put in, less taken out          | Σ deposits − Σ withdrawals, each in euros at its own rate                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                              |
| Cost of what you hold           | Σ euro cost of open lots                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                               |
| Unrealised gain                 | Value − cost of open lots, per holding and in all                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                      |
| Realised gain                   | Σ (proceeds after fees − FIFO euro cost) over sales                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                    |
| Income                          | Σ (gross − withheld) ÷ rate, gross and withheld shown too                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                              |
| Account fees                    | Σ fees − Σ refunds, in euros                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                           |
| Currency on cash                | Per foreign currency, the balance's value today − its euro amount in                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                   |
| Total gain                      | Value − what was put in less taken out, which equals unrealised + realised + income − account fees + currency on cash                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                  |
| Total gain %                    | Total gain ÷ what was put in less taken out, hidden when that is zero or less                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                          |
| Yearly return                   | XIRR: the r at which Σ flowᵢ ÷ (1 + r)^(daysᵢ ÷ 365) and today's value balance, flows being deposits and withdrawals. Hidden for the first year                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                        |
| Last day                        | Value at D − value at D′, as "Last day" above, and that over the earlier value                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                         |
| Price and currency parts        | Price part = q × (p₁ − p₀) ÷ x₁ per open lot, p₀ its cost per unit with fees, both in the quote currency. Currency part = the gain less the price part, so the two add up exactly                                                                                                                                                                                                                                      |
| Average cost                    | Cost of open lots in the quote currency ÷ open quantity                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                |
| Weight, allocation              | Holding, class or currency value ÷ value                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                               |
| Dividend yield                  | Income of the last 12 months ÷ value                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                   |
| Fees paid                       | Σ trading and exchange fees in euros. Shown, never subtracted again                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                    |
| Time-weighted return            | Split at every deposit and withdrawal day. Each piece rᵢ = (V at its end − the flow of that day) ÷ V at its start − 1, V at the close including that day's flows, chained as Π(1 + rᵢ) − 1. For a period of a year or more also (1 + r)^(365 ÷ days) − 1 a year, so 10% over two years is 4.88% a year. Under a year only the total                                                                                    |
| Period start                    | A period starts at the close of its first day. One that starts on the first deposit day, as All always does, starts just before that day's records instead: everything put in that day counts as put in, and the day's fees and trades count inside the period. So Performance All gives the Overview's yearly return                                                                                                  |
| Benchmark return                | The benchmark's own close in euros at the period's end ÷ its close at the start − 1, in today's units, with no flows. It is set against the portfolio's time-weighted return, and the difference is the portfolio's less the benchmark's                                                                                                                                                                               |
| The same money in the benchmark | The same deposits and withdrawals on the same days into the benchmark at its closes, then its end value and XIRR beside the portfolio's, and the gap in euros. A withdrawal larger than the benchmark's value sells all of it, and Performance says so. Only these figures come from the replay. Its own time-weighted return stands still while it holds nothing, so after a full sale it no longer follows the index |
| Largest fall                    | The deepest drop from a running peak of the time-weighted index at the growth line's points: the period's first day, each month end and its last day. A deposit or withdrawal day splits the return but is no point, so a dip on it shows neither on the chart nor here                                                                                                                                                |
| Volatility                      | Sample standard deviation (n − 1) of the monthly time-weighted returns × √12, from 12 of them. The months run back from the period's last day to the same day of each month before it, so a complete year and 1Y both have 12, though 1Y from 29 Oct holds only 11 whole calendar months. Below 12 it is empty, and each year row shows its own when the year has 12                                                   |

**The job.** Its code lives in `src/apps/investments/prices/job/`, pure TypeScript behind four ports (fetch a URL, read a Drive file by id, overwrite a file's content by id, read a script property), so vitest covers it like the rest. A second Vite entry in library mode bundles it into one IIFE for Apps Script, and a small hand-written `price-job/Triggers.js` is the only code that touches `UrlFetchApp`, `DriveApp` and `PropertiesService`. `npm run build:price-job` runs in CI so it keeps compiling. The job never parses a figure: closes stay text as the providers send them, so it needs neither `bigint` nor `Intl`, whose support in Apps Script's V8 runtime Google's pages do not state (unconfirmed). `tests/importBoundaries.spec.ts` keeps the job's imports to the prices format and its own code, so they stay out. The owner deploys it with `npx clasp push`, and `.clasp.json` is gitignored. Each day at 06:00 to 07:00 Europe/Zagreb:

1. Open `kuna-investments.json` and `kuna-investments-prices.json` by the two file ids the owner stores as script properties at setup, and stop with a note when either cannot be opened. Ids avoid a search by name, which matches any file of that name, and Drive's `appProperties` are "restricted to your app" (Google's properties guide, loaded 30 Oct), so the job cannot see the mark Kuna finds its files by.
2. Read only what it needs from the Investments file: each instrument's source and source symbol, the currencies records name, and each instrument's first transaction date. Stop with a note when the file's schema is newer than the job knows.
3. Fetch the latest closes per source, the history of any new series from its first transaction, the ECB series of USD, GBP and CHF from 2015 plus any other currency the file names, and the split factors Tiingo reports.
4. Merge into each series and overwrite the prices file with `File.setContent`, which "overwrites the content of the file", "throws an exception if content is larger than 10MB" and needs the `https://www.googleapis.com/auth/drive` scope (Apps Script reference, loaded 30 Oct). So the script holds full access to the owner's Drive, which the README and the privacy page say. Log counts and failures only, never a symbol or a figure.

Budget: one Tiingo call per US listing, one Alpha Vantage call per European listing and one for the ECB. Apps Script allows 20,000 URL fetches a day and 6 minutes a run (lane 4). The README tells the owner to run the job once from the editor after setup, so the rates are there before the first import.

A series is keyed by source and source symbol, so two instruments on one listing share it. Closes are decimal text, every trading day, never thinned.

**How Kuna reads it.** `prices/infrastructure/googlePricesDrive.ts` looks for `kuna-investments-prices.json` by name in the Investments file's folder. Where two devices created one at the same moment, every device takes the one whose id sorts first. Where there is none, it creates the empty file there, so `drive.file` covers it. A Prices step in the app's sync group (`sync/presentation/investmentsSyncGroup.ts`, shaped like `loansSyncGroup.ts`) compares the head revision with the cached one and downloads only on a change. The copy is kept in this browser in its own Dexie table, as `workbookCache.ts` keeps the books, so figures show at once and offline. The page's sync runs only for files on Drive (`src/shared/sync/presentation/pageSync.ts:106`, `:122`), which is the only place a prices file exists. One tab at a time fetches and writes it, behind a Web Lock, and a device that finds it already written today only reads it. The fake build reads a made-up prices file from the fake Drive, and calls no outside service.

**History and charts.** Value, gains, income and the yearly return need only the latest closes. Value over time needs values on month ends. Performance needs them on month ends, on every deposit and withdrawal day, and on the same day of each month back from the period's last day. US listings have daily history from Tiingo. Xetra and Amsterdam have daily closes only from 100 days before the job's first run, and month ends before that if Alpha Vantage's monthly series works on a free key. A day without a close uses the latest one before it, and Performance names the holdings valued at a month-end close for a period. Where a holding has no close at all for a month, the chart starts at the first month every holding has one and says which holdings held it back. One month of EODHD at $19.99 would fill the European gap (lane 4), the owner's call at any time. The benchmark should be a US listing for the same reason: its daily history reaches the first deposit, and Kuna converts it to euros like any holding.

### Import

`imports/domain/portfolioWorkbook.ts` reads the Transactions tab by header name with `readEveryTab` (`src/shared/xlsx/sheetGrids.ts:35`). The other three tabs are the owner's working notes and are not read.

- **Per row:** `Date` (an Excel serial, through `civilDateFromSerial`, `src/shared/calendar/civilDate.ts:27`), `Time`, `Broker`, `Account`, `Type`, `Symbol (Yahoo)`, `Broker symbol`, `ISIN`, `Quantity (as executed)`, `Price (as executed)`, `Currency`, `Gross amount`, `Commission`, `Net cash`, `FX counter currency`, `FX counter amount`, `Revolut FX rate` and `Note`. The split-adjusted columns and `Split factor` are only read to propose split ratios and to check. `Source file` is shown in the dry run.
- **Signs and totals.** Quantities and amounts are signed as the brokers booked them, and commission is positive. `Net cash` is the statement's total, as the owner's Open Items tab says, so a buy's fees are |net cash| − quantity × price and a sale's are quantity × price − net cash. The dry run lists the rows where that differs from `Commission` by more than a cent (some Revolut rows today, where the commission was worked out).
- **Number cells** arrive as numbers, and a few dozen carry binary noise such as a trailing `…0000002`. The reader rounds quantities, prices and rates to twelve places and amounts to cents, half away from zero, and lists any cell where rounding moved more than that noise.
- **Per account,** each distinct `Account` becomes an account the owner names. The raw text only feeds the key.
- **Per instrument,** found by ISIN where the rows give one, else by Yahoo symbol, with a guess filled in: class, name, quote currency from the rows, and price source with its symbol (bare to Tiingo's ticker, `.DE` to Alpha Vantage's `.DEX`, `.AS` to a suffix to check). An existing instrument with the same ISIN or symbol is offered first.
- **Pairing.** Each WITHHOLDING TAX row joins the income of the same account and day whose description it repeats. Each TRANSFER OUT joins the TRANSFER IN with the same instrument and quantity, and each INTERNAL CASH TRANSFER its opposite. An unpaired row waits.
- **Rows it asks about:** each split's ratio, proposed from the shares added ÷ the shares held and confirmed as whole numbers, the spin-off's cost share, proposed from its quantity × price ÷ the parent's open cost, and every Revolut dividend, which Revolut books net of US tax (Open Items), so its gross equals its net with a note, until the owner types the withholding from the PDF.
- **Exchanges** come from `Net cash` and `FX counter amount`, which is net of the euro commission, as the rows' notes say.
- **The key** of each record is `workbook | account | type | date | time | broker symbol | quantity | price | net cash`, folded and hashed with the fact key that moves from Company (`tabularStatement.ts:274-278`), plus its count among equal rows, since two referral credits share every fact. The IBKR and Revolut readers later build the same key from the same facts, so a statement that overlaps the workbook adds nothing.
- **The dry run** (`imports/domain/importDryRun.ts`) groups the rows: new, already in Kuna, waiting, skipped, and in Kuna from an earlier import of the workbook but gone from it. The last group is listed for the owner to keep or delete, never deleted. A row edited in the workbook shows once as new and once as gone, side by side.
- **Rates** for foreign rows come from the prices file's ECB series. A row whose day the series does not cover waits, and the dry run says to let the job run first. With USD always fetched from 2015, none of the owner's rows should wait.

Then, in order, each when the owner has a real file to read:

1. **IBKR activity statement, CSV,** the format the owner downloads. Its sections are read by name: Trades (`DataDiscriminator, Asset Category, Currency, Symbol, Date/Time, Quantity, T. Price, C. Price, Proceeds, Comm/Fee, Basis, Realized P/L, MTM P/L, Code`, with no trade id and no ISIN), Financial Instrument Information (the ISIN as `Security ID`, joined by symbol), Dividends with Withholding Tax, Deposits & Withdrawals, Fees, Interest and Transfers. Forex rows in Trades become exchanges, and "Inter-Company" transfers become transfers between the two IBKR accounts. The key is the facts as above. A page on GitHub Pages cannot call IBKR's Flex service, which sent no CORS header (lane 1), so Kuna reads a file the owner downloads.
2. **Revolut trading statement, CSV:** `Date, Ticker, Type, Quantity, Price per share, Total Amount, Currency, FX Rate`, with types BUY - MARKET, SELL - MARKET, DIVIDEND, CASH TOP-UP, CASH WITHDRAWAL, CUSTODY FEE and STOCK SPLIT. Its decode moves Company's Windows-1250 fallback to `src/shared/files/csvText.ts` (`src/apps/company/imports/infrastructure/importFiles.ts:30-37`), as does the IBKR reader, whichever lands first.
3. **A Croatian money market fund** needs no parser: a buy is "paid X, got Y units at P" from the fund's confirmation, and the NAV goes in as a `price`.
4. **Trading 212, Kraken, Coinbase** only when the owner names an account there. Their columns are unconfirmed or untested (lane 1).

Until a reader exists, a newer version of the workbook imports only its new rows.

## Risks

- **Free sources move.** Alpha Vantage's Amsterdam coverage and its free monthly series are unconfirmed, and whether the providers answer requests from Google's servers is untested. The job notes each failure, keeps yesterday's closes, and any holding can fall back to a hand price.
- **The workbook is the owner's own merge.** Its Open Items carry over: the spin-off's cost, Revolut commissions worked out from totals, Revolut dividends booked net of US tax, a small cash balance that no statement confirms, and splits that fell on days nothing was held. The import shows each, cash below zero flags a missing row, and the waiting rows keep a holding honest until the owner adds what is missing.
- **A missing split record** makes a holding's quantity and its history wrong around the split. The job's split notes catch the ones Tiingo reports. European listings get no split feed, so the owner adds those by hand.
- **The job reads Kuna's file format.** A deployed job older than the app could misread a newer schema. It reads only a few fields, refuses a newer schema and Kuna shows its note, and CI builds the job on every push so it never falls behind the code.
- **Tax readings are unconfirmed:** the currency reading, FIFO across brokers, losses on lots over two years, the anniversary day, the tax on referral credits and stock yield income, the 2027 deadline. Kuna labels any tax figure as a preparation for the owner and the owner's accountant.
- **Shared origin.** Every project site of the account shares `https://ipazanin.github.io`, valore included (lane 2). Wherever the Investments file lives, the browser keeps a whole copy in IndexedDB (`workbookCache.ts:18-19` names a `kuna-workbook-<id>` database), and so does the prices cache, so script on any of those sites can read the holdings. The defences are keeping every site on that origin free of third-party script, or serving Kuna from its own origin with a custom domain.
- **Privacy slips.** A real figure in a fixture, a screenshot or a commit is the worst failure here. The fake portfolios are written by hand, visual checks run only on fake sets, the job logs counts only, and the grep above runs before each push.

## Decisions for the owner

### How prices reach Kuna

This decides what the Overview header and every value show. Say you open Kuna on your phone on Friday at 08:00 and want Thursday's closes for the S&P 500 fund on Xetra, a US share and the dollar.

- **Run a daily job in your Google account (recommended).** By 07:00 a script in your account has written Thursday's closes into `kuna-investments-prices.json` beside your file, and Kuna reads it with the access it already has. The page talks to no new host and nothing about your holdings leaves Google and the providers. Zagreb prices can join later. Cost: a one-time setup (`clasp push`, two free keys and two file ids in the script's properties, one run, one trigger), the script holds full access to your Drive because Apps Script's file write requires it, it works only with the Investments file on Drive, and providers answering Google's servers is untested until the first run.
- **Publish prices from a GitHub job.** A scheduled workflow fetches and publishes a prices file, which also works for a file kept in this browser. Cost: the list of instruments you hold or held is public in a public repo and its daily file (your history has 29), Zagreb prices may not be republished under ZSE's terms so they stay by hand, GitHub turns a schedule off after 60 days without activity in the repo, and the CSP gains a host.
- **Fetch prices in the page when it opens.** No schedule and nothing public. Cost: you type each key on every device, the CSP gains a host per provider, ZSE and Tiingo send no CORS header so Zagreb prices and US history are out, two devices share Alpha Vantage's 25 calls a day, and the privacy page names every provider.

Which should it be?

### Whether Kuna keeps cash per account

This decides what Value includes, what the % is measured on, and which workbook rows import. Made-up history: you deposited €10,000.00 in 2020 and bought a fund with it, sold it for €16,000.00 in 2023, bought €10,000.00 of another fund in 2024, and it is worth €11,000.00 today. €6,000.00 waits at the broker. Your gain is €7,000.00 either way.

- **Keep cash per account (recommended).** Deposits, withdrawals, fees, exchanges and cash transfers are records, and every record moves its account's cash. The tile reads "+€7,000.00, +70.0% on the €10,000.00 you put in", and the €6,000.00 at the broker is in the value. A dollar purchase is paid from the dollars an exchange brought in, so nothing is counted twice. The time-weighted return and the benchmark use deposits and withdrawals as flows, as lane 3 designed them. Cost: two more kinds (`cash-movement`, `exchange`), every future deposit has to be imported or typed, and a missing row shows as cash below zero until it is found. Your workbook already has the 100 deposits, 9 withdrawals, 37 fees and 24 exchanges.
- **Record only trades.** The tile reads "+€7,000.00, +35.0% on the €20,000.00 you paid for everything you bought", and money waiting at the broker is not in the value. The import skips the workbook's 139 deposit, withdrawal, exchange and cash transfer rows and says so, because a dollar purchase is then a fresh euro outflow, so an exchange recorded as well would count the same money twice: €1,000.00 changed into $1,100.00 and spent on $1,100.00 of shares would show as €2,000.00 held. Dollars kept outside any trade are an instrument priced at 1 in its own currency. The time-weighted return then treats every buy and sale as a flow. The % reads low for someone who sells and buys again, which the yearly return beside it corrects.

Cash can be added later as new kinds without changing a trade, but the import and the figures are simpler built for it from the start. Which should Kuna start with?

### Whether Kuna prepares the tax figures

This decides whether a Tax screen exists. Any part of a 2026 sale from lots under two years old is taxed at 12% and goes on a JOPPD you file yourself, due by the last day of February 2027. That is a Sunday, and the administrative procedure act moves a deadline that falls on a Sunday to the next working day (ZUP Art. 81(2), loaded on zakon.hr 30 Oct), so 1 March 2027 (Porezna's notice for 2027 is still to come). Each leg converts at HNB's middle rate of its day (Art. 12(8)), which on 30 Oct HNB's API showed is the ECB rate of the working day before, so tax never uses a record's stored rate. Every holding's page shows when each lot turns tax-free either way, and the sell form says how many units would be taxable, since the FIFO lots give that for free.

- **Prepare the year's figures (recommended).** A Tax screen lists each sale with the lots it closed, taxable and tax-free, losses of the same year, the tax, the JOPPD figures to type, foreign dividends for INO-DOH, and exports the register. Cost: stage 12, and four answers from your accountant first. Is a dollar sale's gain converted leg by leg (€1.81 of tax in lane 3's example) or once on the sale day (€4.96)? Does FIFO run across brokers? Do losses on lots over two years offset taxable gains? How are referral credits and stock yield income taxed? Built after Performance, in time for February 2027.
- **Leave the year's figures out.** You and your accountant work the JOPPD out from Kuna's holdings and transactions. Cost: none to build, and the tax-free dates still help you time a sale.

Should Kuna prepare the year's tax figures?
