## 1. Category sets

### How the apps handle categories

- **Levels.** Almost every app uses two levels: a group with categories inside it. YNAB says categories "can't be nested inside another category". They live in category groups and you can move them between groups. Actual has groups and categories, and the same actions apply to both. Copilot has groups whose subcategories can have their own budgets, plus an "Unassigned Budget" at group level that covers subcategories you don't budget one by one, or absorbs their overages. Lunch Money calls its two levels category groups and subcategories, and subcategories inherit their group's properties. Monarch has three layers, "type, group, and category", where the type is fixed, flexible or non-monthly, and it starts you with about 60 default categories. Wallet ships fixed main categories that you can rename, with subcategories and sub-subcategories (the fixed part is unconfirmed). Toshl and Firefly are the outliers. Toshl gives each entry one required category and any number of tags. Firefly keeps categories flat and puts amounts on budgets, because categories "cannot be monetized".
- **Tags for cross-cutting things.** Wallet added Labels because category-based budgets were "too complicated". A label can mark every expense from one trip across categories. YNAB's multi-currency guide suggests a memo tag like `#2025beach` for trips. Toshl uses tags for people and occasions. So the apps keep travel and events out of the category tree.
- **How many.** YNAB says there is no right number ("some people have 45, some people have 145"). It advises starting small and letting new categories "earn their way into your budget". Lunch Money also says to start with general categories and add detail later.
- **Renaming.** Every app lets you rename freely. In YNAB, a credit card payment category takes its name from the account.
- **Merging.** None of the apps has a merge button. YNAB and Actual both merge by deleting a category and moving its history to another. YNAB: deleting a category with past activity makes you "reassign that activity to another category". Actual: on delete "you will be presented with a box to select which category the balance and/or transactions should be moved to". YNAB recommends hiding a category with activity, because hiding is "much less destructive". Actual's hidden categories "still impact your budget". Toshl can merge tags into categories, but not tags into tags.
- **Income.** Income gets its own track in every app. Actual has exactly one income group, and it can't be deleted. Lunch Money marks a category "Treat as income", and every other category counts as spending. Copilot has an Income type that "aren't included in your spending budgets". Firefly records income as deposits from revenue accounts. YNAB sends income to Ready to Assign rather than a category. That is the envelope model and I did not load a page on it.
- **Excluded categories.** Copilot lets you mark a category Excluded, for example "Work Expenses", to keep it out of spending totals. Lunch Money has "Exclude from budget" and "Exclude from totals" as separate switches, and its transfer category has both on. This matters for the owner, who owns a d.o.o. and will sometimes pay for company things from a personal account.

### Set C: needs, wants and savings as a second view

The idea: do not build a separate tree. Give each group (or subcategory) one flag, Need (Nužno) or Want (Želje). Savings is income minus spending, plus transfers to savings and investments. One chart then shows the 50/30/20 split for any month. Lunch Money recommends grouping by necessity and offers a "Needs, Wants, Savings" starter. Monarch's fixed, flexible and non-monthly types are the same trick on a different axis.

Suggested flags for Set B: Need for Food/Groceries, Home, Transport, Health, Money and fees. Want for Restaurants, Coffee, Delivery, Boat, Personal, Leisure, Travel, Gifts. These are judgement calls, and the owner should be able to flip them.

## 2. Budgeting methods

### Zero-based envelopes (YNAB, Actual's default)

- **How it works.** You budget only money you already have. In Actual you assign it to categories until "To Budget" reaches 0. Unspent money "simply rolls over into the category's balance next month".
- **Overspending.** YNAB takes red (cash) overspending out of the next month's Ready to Assign, and it does this automatically. Actual takes "all overspending" out of next month's To Budget and resets the category to zero, unless you switch on "Rollover overspending" for that category. YNAB treats credit card overspending (yellow) separately, and it does not roll over the same way.
- **What it costs in screens.** You need a monthly assignment grid, a Ready to Assign figure, money moves between categories, overspending colours, a month-end carry rule, and for cards a payment category per card. Every euro of income has to be assigned before the month makes sense.
- **Fit for the owner.** Poor. The owner wants to see where money goes. Envelopes are a planning discipline and they turn the app into a chore if the owner doesn't want to plan.

### Simple monthly limits per category (Copilot, Spendee, Wallet, Firefly, Actual's tracking budget)

- **How it works.** Set a limit on some categories and compare spending against it. Actual's tracking budget "won't automatically rollover funds from month to month". If a category is overdrawn you raise its budget. Projected savings is budgeted income minus budgeted spending. Copilot can carry the leftover or the overspend per category when you turn rollovers on, "over or under spend will accumulate month over month". You can switch it off for fixed bills like internet. Spendee alerts at 75% and 90% and shows a suggested daily spend. Firefly budgets count only withdrawals, and you can't fund a budget from income.
- **What it costs in screens.** One list of limits with progress bars, one setting per category, one alert rule. No assignment, no Ready to Assign.
- **Fit.** Good. This is the plainest method that still answers "am I over on eating out this month?".

### 50/30/20

- **How it works.** Split after-tax income into about 50% needs, 30% wants and 20% savings and debt repayment. This is a secondary source only, see Unconfirmed.
- **What it costs in screens.** Only the Need or Want flag from Set C, plus one chart comparing the actual split with the target. There are no per-category limits.
- **Fit.** It works as an analytics view, not as a budget. It costs almost nothing once groups carry the flag.

### Flexible budget (Monarch flex)

- **How it works.** There are three buckets. Fixed holds bills that stay the same each month. Non-monthly holds annual renewals and other irregular costs, so that "large purchases don't mess with your flex number". Flexible is one lump sum for everything else, "based on your previous average spending", for example the last three months divided by three. The formula is income minus fixed minus non-monthly minus goals. The Monarch help center (unconfirmed) says that with flex you move money only to and from the flexible bucket as a whole.
- **What it costs in screens.** One "left to spend this month" number, a fixed flag on categories, and a non-monthly list. It is cheap to build, and it fits people who "don't need to track every purchase".
- **Fit.** It's a strong contender because it gives one number. But the owner wants to see where money goes by category, and flex deliberately hides that.

### Sinking funds for yearly costs

- **How it works.** YNAB's yearly target: "Assign $600 for taxes by next year and the target will ask you for $50 each month". It repeats after the "By" month. "Set aside another" keeps adding the full amount, while "Refill up to" tops up only what was spent. Monarch's non-monthly bucket serves the same job. Copilot does it with a rollover on one category. Firefly's piggy banks are tied to a real savings account and filled only by transfers. Monarch goals (June 2026 blog) allocate from any account, carry a target date and planned monthly contribution, and show on-track status with a forecast.
- **What it costs in screens.** A category with "yearly amount and due month" turns on rollover for itself only. It shows "set aside so far" against "due". There is no real money movement unless the user also transfers to a savings account.
- **Fit.** Worth having in a light form. Car registration, car insurance (AO, kasko), boat berth and boat registration are exactly the bills that make a normal month look fine and one month look terrible.

### Recommendation

Use monthly limits on the groups the owner picks, with no rollover by default. Add a "yearly cost" kind of category that accrues one twelfth per month and carries its balance. Skip envelopes. Show 50/30/20 as a chart from the Need and Want flag, not as a budget. If the owner later wants Monarch's single number, "left to spend" is just total limits minus spending so far. That needs no new rules.

## 4. Manual entry on a phone

What the apps do:

- **Amount first.** YNAB: "Enter the amount of the transaction using the number pad and tap Done", then pick the type (Spending, Inflow, Transfer). Toshl: "enter the amount, select a category and click Save". Both open a number pad as the first screen.
- **Payee remembers the category.** YNAB assigns a payee's most recent category automatically. It changes the default only "when 2 of the 3 most recent transactions use a different category", and the mobile app offers the next five categories used with that payee.
- **Last-used defaults.** Toshl keeps the last-used currency as the default. YNAB offers an add button on Home, Spending, Plan and on each account. The Plan tab version starts from a category, which is a fast path for "I just spent on X". Toshl hides the account picker when there is only one account. The date defaults to today.
- **Splits.** Enter the total, then add lines with category and amount. The remainder is shown and can be distributed. YNAB splits evenly if every line is 0. Actual's Distribute spreads the remainder evenly over empty lines, or in proportion if every line has an amount. YNAB records cash back at a till as a transfer line inside the split.
- **Repeating.** YNAB has repeating schedules on any transaction. Toshl repeats daily, weekly, monthly, yearly or custom, marks repeats with an "R" (snippet), and warns that deleting all repetitions deletes past entries too. Actual can auto-add a schedule's transaction on the date or wait for approval, and its "upcoming length" decides how far ahead they show.
- **Photo.** YNAB lets you attach a photo on the Plan tab entry. Spendee's Magic AI Scan fills price, category and labels from a receipt photo. Its help page shows no review step, but Kuna's should have one (receipt AI is another lane).
- **Cash.** Toshl treats ATM withdrawals as transfers: "This is still your money, the transfer merely moved it from one pile of money to the other". Spendee gives cash withdrawal as its transfer example. Wallet sells manual entry mainly for cash. So the common model is a Cash account, a transfer from the bank to Cash at the ATM, and spending entered against Cash.

Proposed flow for Kuna: a big "+" opens a number pad. Type the amount and tap a category from a short grid of the most used (or type a payee, which suggests its category). The account defaults to the last-used account, or to Cash if the owner prefers. Save. Date, note, tag, split, repeat and photo sit one tap away, never in the way.

## 5. Transfers and savings

- **One transfer, two legs, no category.** YNAB transfers between two accounts of the same kind need no category. Actual links the two halves so "updating one automatically updates the other", and the transfer has no category when both accounts are on-budget. Copilot's Internal Transfer type is "excluded from spending budgets". Toshl transfers have no categories or tags and stay out of sums as long as both accounts are visible. Spendee: "Transfers are excluded from expense and income categories", "They are, however, counted in the Cash Flow!".
- **Leaving the tracked world.** Money that goes to an account the app doesn't track (Spendee's "Out of Spendee", YNAB's tracking or loan accounts, Actual's off-budget accounts) needs a category on the tracked side, because it really does leave. For Kuna this is the investment and loan question in the decisions below.
- **Detection on import.** Toshl detects transfers and ATM withdrawals in bank feeds automatically. Lunch Money users ask for the same (feedback board). Pairing the OTP outflow with the Revolut inflow is the statement lane's job, but the product rule is that a matched pair becomes one transfer.
- **Savings goals.** There are three shapes. (a) YNAB: a category with a target, virtual money inside the budget. (b) Firefly piggy bank: tied to one real savings account, filled only by transfers, several per account. (c) Monarch goal: allocates from any accounts, target date, planned monthly contribution, on-track status and projected finish date. For "see where money goes", the plainest is a savings account with an optional target and date on the account itself. That shows progress as the balance against the target, and it needs no allocation model.

## 6. Multi-currency

- **Monarch:** no real support. It shows "$" for USD or CAD and does no conversion (help article, unconfirmed).
- **YNAB:** one currency per budget, with a separate budget per currency. A foreign card spend on a home-currency account is entered at the amount the bank shows. Or you estimate it and flag it, then fix it when it clears.
- **Actual:** "currency agnostic and does not support multi-currency". The workaround is rules that convert at a fixed rate and note the original amount.
- **Firefly:** budgets count only the main currency, and "foreign amounts are ignored".
- **Toshl:** keeps the foreign amount, suggests the day's rate, lets you type your own rate, and shows the main-currency value in details and totals.
- **Lunch Money:** a primary currency for all totals, a currency dropdown on every amount, historic daily rates, the closest day's rate if one is missing. Showing the rate used is marked done on its feedback board (unconfirmed).

What this means for Kuna: two different cases.

1. **A euro account pays in a foreign currency** (an OTP card abroad, or Revolut converting at the till). The euro amount the bank charged is the truth. The original amount and currency are display-only, shown as "£12.40 · €14.62".
2. **Money held in a foreign currency** (a Revolut GBP or USD pocket). That pocket is an account in its own currency. Its transactions are in that currency, and totals convert them to euros at the transaction date's rate (ECB reference rate is the obvious source). An exchange between pockets is a transfer with two amounts, and the gap between the market rate and the amounts is the fee.

## 7. Shared spending with a partner

It is common and the apps sell it. Monarch's Shared Views (October 2025) tags accounts and transactions as "mine, theirs, or ours" and filters reports by owner, but "we don't currently offer a way to budget separately". Spendee's shared wallets let both people edit, use the owner's categories, keep budgets per user, and can't share bank-connected wallets. YNAB points couples to Splitwise and a "Splitting" category (unconfirmed). Not in the owner's list. If it ever comes, an owner tag on accounts and transactions is the cheapest start, and nothing in v1 needs to change for it.

## Proposal for v1, plainest first

### Screens

1. **Add** (phone first). Number pad, category grid with the most-used on top, payee field with remembered category, account (last used), Save. One tap away: date, note, tag, split, repeat, photo.
2. **Transactions.** A list by day with filters for month, account, category, payee and tag. Tap to edit, swipe to delete. Transfers show once, with both accounts.
3. **Accounts.** Each account with its balance: OTP current, Revolut (one per currency held), savings, Cash. Tap for the account's own list. Includes an adjust-balance action, which writes an "unrecorded" correction line and is used mostly for Cash.
4. **This month (home).** Total spent against income, spending by group with drill-down, and budget bars for groups that have a limit. "Left to spend" sits at the top if any limits exist.
5. **Trends.** Twelve-month bars per group or category, income against spending per month, top payees.
6. **Budgets.** One row per group: monthly limit or none. Yearly-cost categories show the yearly amount, due month, set-aside-so-far and due.
7. **Categories.** Two levels, rename, reorder, hide, delete-into-another (that is merge), and Need or Want flag. Income categories sit in their own fixed group. One "excluded" kind of category is there for company or reimbursable spending.

Later, not v1: recurring detection screen, year-over-year, savings goals beyond a target on a savings account, shared ownership.

### Rules

- Every transaction is spending, income or transfer. Spending and income need a category. A transfer has none.
- A transfer between two Kuna accounts never counts as spending or income. It shows in each account's cash flow.
- A refund is negative spending in the original category, so the category total goes down rather than income going up. Copilot's "Regular" type covers both spend and refund.
- Budgets are monthly limits on calendar months. Leftover and overspend do not carry over, except for yearly-cost categories, which accrue one twelfth a month and carry their balance.
- A split's lines must add up to the total, and each line has its own category.
- Deleting a used category always asks where its transactions go. Hiding keeps history.
- The euro amount is what counts. Foreign amounts are shown beside it. Foreign-currency accounts convert at the transaction date's rate for totals.
- Cash is an account. An ATM withdrawal is a transfer into it.
- Income and spending inside Kuna mean personal money only. Company money stays in the Company module.

### Decisions for the owner

**1. Flat or two-level categories**

The situation: on the Add screen you tap a category for every coffee and every fuel stop. Example: €3.20 at a café. In a flat set you tap "Eating out" once. In two levels you tap Food and then Coffee, or tap Coffee directly if it is in the most-used grid.

- _Keep one flat level of 12._ Fastest entry and simplest charts. You can't tell coffee from restaurants without filtering by payee.
- _Use groups with subcategories._ Budgets and the home screen work on groups, while trends can drill into Coffee against Restaurants. It costs a grid of about 40 subcategories and a group step in the category editor.

Recommendation: two levels (Set B), with the most-used subcategories pinned on the Add screen so entry stays one tap.

**2. How cash is tracked**

The situation: you take €100 from an ATM and spend it over two weeks at the market and the bakery. Example: €100 out on 3 November, €18 at the pazar on 5 November.

- _Treat the withdrawal as spending._ Book the €100 to a "Cash" category and stop. Nothing to maintain, but the spending report shows "Cash €100" instead of groceries.
- _Keep a Cash account._ The withdrawal is a transfer, and each purchase is entered against Cash with its real category. Reports are accurate. It costs entering cash buys and sometimes correcting the wallet balance with an adjust line.

Recommendation: a Cash account, with the adjust-balance action so a forgotten €5 never blocks anything.

**3. Rollover of leftover money**

The situation: your Eating out limit is €200. In October you spent €150. Example: does November start at €200 or €250?

- _No rollover._ Every month starts fresh. Easy to read, and a heavy month does not punish the next one.
- _Rollover per category._ Unspent and overspent amounts carry forward, as Copilot does. It suits people who save up inside a category, but the budget numbers stop being obvious.

Recommendation: no rollover, except yearly-cost categories, which need it to work.

**4. Yearly costs**

The situation: car registration and insurance come once a year. Example: €480 every April. With plain monthly totals, April looks €480 worse than March.

- _Show them where they fall._ Simplest. Trends spike in April.
- _Yearly-cost categories._ You set €480 due in April, and the app shows €40 a month set aside and how much is covered so far. No real money moves.
- _Real sinking fund._ Same as above, but you also transfer €40 a month to a savings account and the app tracks that account against the target. Most accurate, and it needs a transfer every month.

Recommendation: yearly-cost categories without real money movement. They cover car and boat costs well and add one setting per category.

**5. Loan instalments and investment transfers**

The situation: Kuna already has Loans and Investments. Example: a €420 monthly loan instalment from the current account, and €250 a month sent to a broker.

- _Instalment counts as spending._ Book the full €420 as spending under a "Loan instalments" category. "Where money goes" shows it plainly. Principal and interest stay the Loans module's business.
- _Instalment as a transfer to the loan._ Only the interest counts as spending. More correct, but it needs a link to the Loans module and a principal and interest split per payment.
- _Investment transfers._ Count them as saving (a transfer out, shown in the savings rate), never as spending. Being a transfer, they need no category.

Recommendation: the full instalment as spending in v1, and investment transfers counted as saving.

**6. Personal money spent on the company**

The situation: you pay a €60 hosting bill for the company from your personal card and the company pays you back later. Example: it shows as spending in October and the payback as income in November.

- _Use an excluded category._ It is called "Za tvrtku / For the company" and it stays out of spending and income, like Copilot's Work Expenses. Reports stay clean, and the payback goes into the same category.
- _Treat it as normal spending and income._ No setup, but both months are distorted.

Recommendation: the excluded category.

**7. Foreign currency held in Revolut**

The situation: you keep £300 in a Revolut GBP pocket for trips. Example: £40 spent in London from that pocket.

- _Track only the euro side._ The pocket isn't an account. Exchanges show as transfers out of the euro account, spending in GBP is invisible until you add it by hand, and balances never match Revolut.
- _Make each held currency its own account._ The £40 is recorded in GBP and shown as about €46 in totals at that day's rate. It costs a currency field on accounts and a daily rate table.

Recommendation: each held currency as its own account, and only for currencies where you actually keep a balance. Card spends on euro accounts stay in euros with the original amount shown beside them.
