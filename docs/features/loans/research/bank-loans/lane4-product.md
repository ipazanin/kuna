# H3 lane 4: how other tools do loans, and Kuna's options

How Firefly III, GnuCash, hledger, Ledger, Actual, YNAB, Monarch, Copilot, Lunch Money, two debt payoff apps and Croatian bank apps model and show loans, and the product options for Kuna's first Loans app with a recommendation for each.

Every page cited below was loaded on 2026-10-28 and the claim was found in its text. Items I could not load or find are marked **unconfirmed**. Loan law and mechanics, the owner's real figures and the codebase belong to the other H3 lanes, so this lane names those questions and leaves them there. Background: the loans sketch in [lane 3 of the hub research](../../../../architecture/research/hub-lane3-apps.md) and the wave list in [the hub proposal](../../../../architecture/hub.md).

All money examples use made-up terms, not the owner's loan: €120,000 at 3.00% fixed over 240 months, first instalment January 2025, which gives an annuity of €665.52.

## In short

1. **Getting the loan in:** enter the terms and paste the bank's repayment plan, with a check that they agree. The bank's rows win wherever they exist.
2. **Planned vs actual:** past instalments count as paid unless the owner marks an exception. Once statements arrive in H6, they confirm instalments by matching.
3. **Rate changes and early repayment:** a dated rate period or extra payment regenerates the plan from that date, and a new bank plan replaces it when it arrives. A what-if panel shows "shorter term" and "lower instalment" side by side and never saves.
4. **The subsidy:** a dated subsidy period that splits each instalment into the owner's share and the state's share. The Overview leads with the owner's share and shows when the subsidy ends and what the owner pays after.
5. **Informal loans:** a list of dated movements with a running balance and a fixed direction, repayment in kind as its own movement kind, no interest and no FIFO settlement.
6. **The Overview:** one card per loan with balance, next payment (your share), payoff date and interest left. Charts and the full plan sit on the loan's own page.
7. **Leave out:** payoff strategies, escrow, bank sync, PDF parsing, interest on informal loans, ledger rows, refinancing as a flow, reminders, and loans with a schedule that someone owes the owner.

### What the comparison says

The tools fall into three groups.

- **Ledgers** (Firefly III, GnuCash, hledger, Ledger, Actual, Copilot, Lunch Money) store a loan as an account whose balance is the sum of its movements. None of them stores a repayment plan. The plan is either absent or a rule that generates guesses. When the guess and the bank disagree, the user edits each transaction.
- **Planners** (YNAB, Monarch, Undebt.it, Debt Payoff Planner) store the terms and project forward from today's balance. Their strength is the what-if: one extra payment, the new date, the interest saved. Their weakness is the past. YNAB admits its interest will drift from the lender's through rounding and compounding, and offers an edit per month.
- **Banks** hold the real figures. PBZ shows the figures a borrower asks about: what is due, what is overdue, what is owed in total, when it ends, what has been paid.

No tool takes the bank's repayment plan as input. The owner has that plan, which is better data than any of these tools work from. That shapes most of the recommendations below.

## 1. Getting the loan in

**Screen:** Add loan, and the loan's plan table.

**Example:** The bank's repayment plan for the example loan has 240 rows. Kuna generates its own plan from the terms. In month 14 Kuna computes interest of €287.94 and the bank might show €287.96. The bank may count actual days in each month, and the first instalment may carry interest for the days between payout and the first due date. Either one makes every row differ by a few cents to a few euros, and the last instalment absorbs the leftover. Which rule the owner's bank uses is a question for the mechanics lane and the owner's data.

**Options:**

- **Enter the terms and let Kuna generate the plan.** Works for any loan with known terms, and what-ifs come free. Cost: Kuna's balance never quite matches the bank's statement, and the owner cannot tell whether a difference is rounding or a wrong rate. This is YNAB's position, and its answer is a manual edit per month.
- **Paste the bank's plan.** Every past and future row matches the bank to the cent, including a subsidy column if the plan has one. Cost: an import for the bank's format, and nothing beyond the pasted rows. No what-if, and after a rate change or an extra payment the plan is wrong until the bank's new plan is pasted.
- **Enter the terms and paste the bank's plan, with a check that they agree.** Kuna keeps both. The bank's rows are the figures shown. The generated plan is used for the future after a change and for what-ifs. At entry, a check compares the two row by row and shows the largest difference and the difference in the final balance. Small differences (under a few euros in total) are rounding and say so. Large ones point to a wrong rate, start date or method, and the screen says which row they start at. Cost: two paths plus a comparison view.

**Recommendation:** enter the terms and paste the bank's plan, with the check. The paste can be copied spreadsheet cells (tab-separated date, instalment, interest, principal, balance) rather than a PDF parser. A plan that already sits in a Sheet then takes one copy and one paste. The terms alone stay allowed, so a loan without a plan still works, with a note that figures are Kuna's estimate.

## 2. Planned vs actual

**Screen:** the loan page (each row's status), the Overview's next payment, the H4 dashboard tile.

**Example:** The 30 November 2026 instalment is due. The bank debits it on 1 December because the 30th falls on a weekend. Or the debit fails because the account is short, and the bank takes it on 8 December with late interest.

**How the tools do it:** hledger's forecasts start after the latest real transaction, so planned and actual never overlap. Firefly III and Actual both say that people who don't import use recurring entries, and people who import match imported rows to an expected payment instead. Actual matches within two days of the expected date. YNAB has you record every payment, even on a linked loan account.

**Options:**

- **Count past instalments as paid.** The balance today is the plan's balance after the last due date that has passed. Zero monthly work, and right for a bank loan on direct debit, which is the normal case. Cost: a missed or late instalment is invisible, and Kuna can only say "due", never "paid".
- **Record each payment by hand.** Kuna knows what was paid and when, so missed and late instalments show. Cost: twelve entries a year for a debit that almost never fails. The GnuCash list shows users give up and adjust instead.
- **Count past instalments as paid until statements confirm them.** In H3 a passed row shows as "due, assumed paid". The owner can mark an exception on a row: missed, paid late on a date, paid in part. An extra payment is its own record (option 3). From H6, an imported statement row on the loan's account matches a due row by amount and a date window of a few days, the way Actual matches schedules. The row becomes "paid", linked to the statement row. A passed row with no match after the window shows as "not seen in your statement".

**Recommendation:** count past instalments as paid until statements confirm them. Five row states cover it: upcoming, due (assumed paid), paid (matched), late, missed. Nothing in H3 depends on H6, and H6 adds certainty without changing the plan. The loan app writes no ledger rows, as lane 3 set out. The match is a computed view.

## 3. Rate changes and early repayment

**Screen:** the loan page (a dated change list under the plan) and a what-if panel.

**Example, rate change:** the fixed period ends and from 1 March 2028 the rate becomes variable at a new figure. The bank recalculates the instalment and sends a new plan.

**Example, extra payment:** in July 2027, after the 31st instalment, the owner pays €10,000 extra on the example loan (balance about €108,000). The bank asks: shorter term or lower instalment?

| Effect           | Instalment | Last payment                     | Interest from July 2027 | Interest saved |
| ---------------- | ---------- | -------------------------------- | ----------------------- | -------------- |
| No extra payment | €665.52    | December 2044                    | €31,131                 |                |
| Shorter term     | €665.52    | December 2042, 24 months earlier | €24,771                 | €6,360         |
| Lower instalment | €604.03    | December 2044                    | €28,280                 | €2,851         |

Figures are a monthly annuity at 3.00%, rounded to the cent each month, made-up terms. The owner's bank may charge a fee or limit how often the owner can do this. That belongs to the law lane.

**How the tools do it:** Monarch stores a rate type (fixed or variable) and a rate, with no history of past rates. YNAB has you edit the rate, and for a refinance close the account and open a new one. GnuCash's assistant takes a rate change frequency, but an extra payment breaks the generated payments. Monarch and Undebt.it both run what-ifs that never touch the real plan, show the old line next to the new one and answer with a date and an interest figure. Undebt.it also solves the reverse question: what monthly amount gets me debt-free by a chosen date.

**Options for a change that has happened:**

- **Add a dated change and let Kuna regenerate from that date.** A rate period (from date, rate, fixed or variable) or an extra payment (date, amount, shorter term or lower instalment). Rows before the date stay. Rows after it are recalculated. Cost: generated rows drift from the bank's new plan by cents, as in option 1.
- **Wait for the bank's new plan and paste it.** Exact. Cost: until it arrives, the loan shows the old, wrong plan.
- **Add the dated change now, and let the bank's new plan replace the rows from its first date.** The check from option 1 runs on the new plan against the change the owner entered.

**Options for a what-if:**

- **A what-if panel that never saves.** Amount, date, and both effects side by side, with new last payment, new instalment, interest saved and months saved. Applying it turns it into a real extra payment record. Cost: one panel.
- **Saved scenarios.** Several named plans to compare later. Cost: storage, a list, and stale scenarios after the next real change.

**Recommendation:** for changes, the dated change now and the bank's plan when it arrives. For what-ifs, one panel that never saves, showing both effects at once, since that is the choice the bank asks the owner to make. A target-date solver can come later.

## 4. The subsidy

**Screen:** the loan card on the Overview, the plan table, the H4 tile, and later the H6 budget.

**Example:** with a made-up subsidy of 30% for the first five years, the €665.52 instalment splits into €465.86 from the owner and €199.66 from the state. In January 2030 the subsidy ends and the owner's debit goes up by about €200 a month. The real share, how it is paid (to the bank, or credited to the owner) and what ends or extends it are for the law and data lanes. Whether the bank's plan shows the split as a column is **unconfirmed**.

**How the tools do it:** none of them model a third party paying part of an instalment. The closest is Firefly III's how-to, which records someone helping to pay off a loan as a separate deposit from that person into the liability. YNAB, Monarch and the payoff apps have nothing for it.

**Options:**

- **Split each instalment into your share and the subsidy's share.** A subsidy period on the loan (from, to, share as a percentage), overridable per row by a subsidy column in the bank's plan. Each row shows total, your share and subsidy. Cost: one record kind. After an extra payment or a rate change, the share is recalculated from the new instalment, which may or may not be how the state computes it (law lane).
- **Treat the state as a second payer.** Each instalment has two payments, one from the owner and one from the state, the way Firefly records a benefactor. H6 matching then sees the owner's debit match the owner's share. Cost: twice the payment records, and a payer concept no other screen uses.
- **Show the full instalment only.** Nothing to build. Cost: for the subsidy years every "you pay" figure is wrong by the state's share, and H6's statement match fails on every row.

**Recommendation:** split each instalment into your share and the subsidy's share. The card leads with what the owner pays, shows the full instalment smaller, and carries one line such as "Subsidy ends January 2030, then €665.52 a month". That line is the figure the owner most needs to plan for. Interest paid stays the bank's figure for the whole instalment, with a note on how much of it the state covered this year.

## 5. Informal loans

**Screen:** the informal loan's page (a movement list) and its card on the Overview.

**Example:** The owner borrowed €5,000 from a private person. The owner repays €300 in some months and nothing in others. Once the owner pays a €450 bill on the lender's behalf, which counts as repayment. Later the owner borrows €1,000 back. No interest, no fixed plan.

**How the tools do it:** Firefly III has the clearest model. A liability is either "I owe" or "I am owed", picked when it is created. Borrowing more is a withdrawal from the liability, either to a bank account or straight to a shop. Paying back is a transfer into it. Interest is optional and entered by hand. Forgiving part of it goes to an expense account. GnuCash keeps a per-person "money owed to you" account. The planners and payoff apps assume a rate and a minimum payment and have no direction.

**How Kuna's company owner-loan book compares:** it reads lent and repaid rows off the categorised company ledger and settles repayments against loans, exact sets first and then oldest first, with a signed balance (lane 3 cites `ownerLoans.ts`). The direction and sign convention fit a family loan well. The rest does not. In H3 there is no personal ledger to read, since that comes with H6. A bill paid on the lender's behalf is not a transfer to the lender at all. And for an interest-free loan, no figure depends on which borrowing a repayment settles.

**Options:**

- **Keep a list of movements with a running balance.** Each movement has a date, an amount, a kind (borrowed, repaid, repaid in kind, adjustment) and a note. Repaid in kind records what the owner paid for and on which date. The direction is fixed per loan and the balance is the signed sum. If the balance goes below zero after a large repayment, the card says "the lender owes you €X" rather than showing a negative debt. Cost: the owner enters each movement. From H6, a money movement can link to its statement row, and a bill paid on the lender's behalf links to that card row and counts as a repayment, not spending.
- **Settle repayments against each borrowing, the way the owner-loan book does.** Shows which borrowing is repaid. Cost: more logic and explanation for figures nobody reads on an interest-free loan. It would matter only if borrowings carried different terms.
- **Add an optional repayment plan and compare.** An agreed monthly amount or a target date, shown as ahead or behind. Cost: a plan the owner does not keep becomes a permanent warning.

**Recommendation:** keep a list of movements with a running balance, with the owner-loan book's direction and sign convention but not its settlement. Leave the plan out until the owner agrees one with the lender. When H5 shows the company's loan to the owner read-only, it uses the same card (balance, last movement, direction), so the two loans without a bank schedule look alike.

## 6. What the Loans Overview shows

**Screen:** the Loans Overview on a phone, and each loan's own page.

**What the others lead with:** Monarch's dashboard leads with four cards: current principal, projected interest, principal plus interest, debt-free date. Then a chart and a list by type. PBZ leads with the instalment, overdue debt, total debt, maturity and the next due date. YNAB's planner leads with interest and time left. Undebt.it answers with a date.

**For one mortgage and one family loan, five figures matter:**

1. What the owner owes today on each loan.
2. The next payment: date and the owner's share.
3. When the subsidy ends, and the instalment after it.
4. The payoff date.
5. Interest left over the life of the loan, and interest paid this year.

A total across loans says little when one is a 20-year bank debt and the other an informal loan, but net worth (H8) needs it. It can be a single line above the cards.

**Phone layout, about 360 px wide:**

```
Loans                                   You owe €120,565
+--------------------------------------------------+
| Housing loan                          €111,358   |
| Next: 30 Nov  €465.86 (of €665.52)               |
| Paid off Dec 2044 . €33,059 interest left        |
| Subsidy ends Jan 2030, then €665.52              |
| [==----------------------]   7% repaid           |
+--------------------------------------------------+
| Loan from a private person             €9,207    |
| Last: 12 Oct, repaid €300                        |
| No fixed plan . you owe                          |
+--------------------------------------------------+
```

(The example loan after its November 2026 instalment, and a made-up informal balance.) Tapping a card opens the loan's page: the same figures on top, a balance-over-time chart (the plan as a line, past rows marked by status from option 2), interest this year and over the life, the subsidy line, the dated changes, the what-if button, then the plan. Five columns do not fit at that width. The plan groups rows by year with yearly totals, opens the current year, and shows date, your share and balance per row. A tap shows the interest and principal split and the status.

**Options:**

- **One card per loan, details on the loan's page.** Everything the owner checks monthly fits one screen. Cost: the chart and the plan are one tap away.
- **Lead with a chart across all loans,** as Monarch does. Good for many debts. Cost: two lines, one of them flat for months, tell the owner nothing they do not already know.
- **Lead with the full plan.** Cost: 240 rows on a phone, and the next payment buried.

**Recommendation:** one card per loan, details on the loan's page. The H4 dashboard tile is the first card's top three lines.
