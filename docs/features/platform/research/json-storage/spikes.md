# JSON storage: Drive spikes

What Drive API v3 did when the owner ran the four spikes on 2026-10-24 in Firefox with the drive.file scope: Keep Forever round trip, two saves in the same second, the 200 kept revision cap and a generated id used twice.

Run by the owner on 2026-10-24 at 15:05 UTC, Firefox 158 on the Mac, `npm run dev`, signed in with `drive.file openid email`. The page worked only in a scratch folder `ledgerboard-spike` it created itself. The page lived in the repo's gitignored `spike.local/` and was never committed.

## Results

1. **Keep Forever round trip works.** A create and a media upload with `keepRevisionForever=true` gave 2 revisions, both `keepForever=true`. The first revision downloaded with `revisions/{id}?alt=media` equals the first upload. The download answered from `www.googleapis.com` with no redirect, so the app's CSP needs no new host.
2. **Racing saves are listed at once, but not always in the order Drive applied them.** Two uploads to one file at the same moment, 5 trials, and 5 more with the second 300 ms late. Both revisions were on the first `revisions.list` right after both uploads returned in 10 of 10, and nothing changed at 1, 3 or 10 s. The two uploads always got different revision ids. **In 1 of 10 the head (`files.get headRevisionId`) was not the last listed revision**: the list orders by `modifiedTime`, and the upload that finished last became the head although its `modifiedTime` was earlier. So the check after a save must compare revision ids against the base and its own upload, and read the head from `files.get`, never assume the last listed revision is the head.
3. **The cap is 200 kept revisions, refused loudly.** Upload 201 with `keepRevisionForever=true` got `403 A maximum of 200 revisions can be kept forever.`, reason `keptRevisionsLimitExceeded`. The upload itself was refused, not stored without Keep Forever. So the unkeep housekeeping (keep at most 100) is required, and a `keptRevisionsLimitExceeded` answer should trigger it and one retry.
4. **A generated id used twice gives 409.** `files/generateIds` works under `drive.file`. A second create with the same id answered `409 A file already exists with the provided ID.`, reason `fileIdInUse`. That gives a true compare-and-swap if the check after the save ever proves not enough: the document names a pre-generated claim id, a save creates the claim file first, and a 409 means another device saved since the read.

## Timing

A media upload of a tiny file took 1.4 to 3 s, usually about 1.6 s. `files.get` and `revisions.list` took about 0.4 to 0.5 s. So a save with its checks is about 2.5 to 3.5 s on a desktop connection before the file grows.

## What this means for stages 2 and 3

- The plan's check before and after holds. Spike 2 shows the racing revision is visible to the check after, so a save never vanishes silently.
- The check after reads the revision list since the base. Any revision that is neither the base nor its own upload is another device's save. It then reads the head with `files.get`. The rule that decides which device re-applies its change on top of the other's must be deterministic for both devices, since either may see itself as second. It must not use list order.
- **The rule as built, corrected by the review of the document homes:** the device whose own upload is the head (read with `files.get` after the list) acts. If another save landed since its base, its upload replaced that save, so it reruns its write on that save and uploads the result, or puts that save back and refuses with its message. A device whose upload is not the head answers saved, because the device that landed later always lists after its own upload and so always sees it. On the fake Drive all 24 two-device interleavings keep both saves with exactly one device acting. The rule below, first decided by the lead, lost a save without a message in 4 of the 24: a device whose upload landed first but carries the later `modifiedTime` can list before the other upload lands. The accepted gap: the device that landed last closes its tab before its rerun, and the other save stays only in a kept revision. Three devices saving at once is not designed.
- **The first rule, superseded:** both devices see the same revision list with the same `modifiedTime` and id. Of the racing revisions since the base, the one that sorts first by `modifiedTime`, then by id, stands as it is. Every device whose own upload sorts later downloads the revision just before its own in that order, runs its write again on it, and uploads. That upload goes through the check after again with the standing revision as its base. Only the later device acts, so the two never re-apply on top of each other. If its change no longer passes the checks, it uploads the revision before its own back as it was and refuses its change with a message. The remaining gap: if the later device's tab closes between its upload and its re-upload, the head can lack the earlier save. That needs two saves in the same second and a tab closed within the next three seconds, and the earlier save is still in a kept revision.
- Keep at most 100 kept revisions, and treat `keptRevisionsLimitExceeded` as a signal to unkeep the oldest and retry once.
- The claim-file compare-and-swap of spike 4 is the fallback, not the plan.
