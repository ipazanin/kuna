# Hub, lane 1: architecture inventory

Read-only research on the HEAD copy of ledgerboard at 7128551. Every path is relative to that copy. Designed on top of the Wave 12b target shape ([the Wave 12b plan](https://github.com/ipazanin/kuna/blob/cd621ab/docs/features/platform/storage-boundary-plan.md)), not only today's code. Counts come from the commands or scratch scripts named beside them. The scripts were in a scratch folder: `imports.mjs` builds the relative-import edge list of `src` (423 files, 3,179 import lines), `agg.mjs` and `cycles.mjs` query it, and `classify.mjs` sorts every file into a class. No web source is cited, so nothing here needed a page load.

## Headline facts

- The shell is already mostly generic. `AppShell.vue`, `AppBrand.vue`, `ScreenNavigation.vue`, `PageTabs.vue`, `DrillLink.vue`, the theme, the PWA worker and the router's scroll and focus behaviour name no company concept. What ties the shell to the company is `App.vue` and two route files: the root component reads the company workbook store, the owner-pay switch and the review count, and the route table is a closed list of eight company screens.
- Of 423 source files: 23 shell, 43 shared kernel (plus 3 dev-only gallery files), 33 storage, 172 company app, 11 files of pack data living in core folders, 138 hr-doo pack.
- The `workbook` feature is two things at once: the storage layer and the company ledger's domain (`Workbook`, `Transaction`, `Category`, buckets). Its storage files import the rules, review and settings features in 21 import lines across 9 files. Wave 12b stages 6 and 7 split most of this, but the `Workbook` aggregate stays the company app's.
- The hr-doo pack is a plug-in of the company app, not a sibling app. It imports `features/workbook` from 76 files and `features/filters` from 37. It should stay under the company app, and the hub needs its own seam one level up.
- Five company assumptions sit in places a hub would share: one store singleton (50 importers), one data-source choice key, one global filter state written into every route, one money format set from the company workbook, and six fixed company profit-and-loss buckets (34 importers).
- Wave 12b's shapes carry over well. `RecordKind`, `RecordStorage` and the contract suite are storage-neutral. The unit of work is atomic only inside one storage, which decides how apps may write across each other. `PackContribution` is the template for an app contribution.

## 3. State and storage

### Today

- **Store.** `createWorkbookStore` (`workbookStore.ts:214`) holds one `WorkbookState` (`:125-140`): the parsed `Workbook`, the raw `snapshot`, the choice, sync status and the session's writes. Writes go through five store methods (`:195-210`), which 12b replaces with `store.write` and a unit of work.
- **Sync.** Cache first, then a full read of every tab (`readFromGoogle.ts:29`, `googleWorkbookPorts.ts:21`). Auto-sync every ten minutes while a token is good (`autoSync.ts:4`).
- **Cache.** One IndexedDB database per workbook, `ledgerboard-workbook-<id>`, version 8, a table per tab keyed by `sheetRow` (`workbookCache.ts:17-26`). Every sync and every write replaces it whole (`:45-54`).
- **The one-Sheet assumption.** One choice (`workbookChoice.ts:4-8`, `:10`), one Picker pick, one `WORKBOOK_TABS` list (`workbookSnapshot.ts:13-30`), and the welcome screen as the first run of the whole app (`App.vue:63`).
- **Google scopes.** `drive.file openid email` (`googleAuth.ts:31`), plus `gmail.compose` as a second in-memory token for invoice mail (README "Google sign-in"). `drive.file` covers files the user picks or the app creates (README line 8-9). Wave 13 adds `drive.readonly` as an in-memory grant (queue, Wave 13).
- **Device keys in `localStorage`.** `ledgerboard.workbook`, `ledgerboard.google-token`, `ledgerboard.google-account`, `ledgerboard.google-redirect`, `ledgerboard.theme`, `ledgerboard.card.*`, `ledgerboard.invoice-mail-draft` (`grep -rn "'ledgerboard[.-]" src`). Only the first is per workbook. Google, theme and card keys are device-wide and fit a hub as they are.
- **Workbook settings keys.** Today's keys already carry informal prefixes: `billing_`, `invoice_`, `hr_`, `import_profile_`, `label_`. Unprefixed: `template_version`, `company_name`, `currency`, `number_locale`, `owner_pay_module`, `review_label`, `card_settlement_ibans` (`settings.ts:460-521`).

### Each app with its own storage

- A choice per storage instead of one key. The cache already keys by workbook id, so a second database works without change once its tables come from the registered kinds instead of `WORKBOOK_TABS`.
- No new Google scope. A personal Sheet is either picked once in the Picker or created by the app, and `drive.file` covers both. Each new Sheet is one more Picker step, or none if the app creates it.
- Sync reads every chosen storage. A sync status per storage, or one status with the oldest time.
- A write stays inside one storage. A Google `batchUpdate` targets one spreadsheet, so a unit of work spanning two Sheets cannot be atomic. Cross-app links have to be references by `RecordId`, read-only from the other side.
- Excel mode exports one xlsx per storage.
- The company Sheet stays company-only. That matters because it is the shadow-accounting Sheet the Apps Script writes to.
- An app nobody set up costs nothing: no tabs, no sync, no first-run screen in the way.

### All apps in one storage

- One pick, one sync, one cache, one export, and a unit of work that can write across apps atomically.
- Tab names need an app prefix so a personal `accounts` or `categories` tab cannot clash with the company's 16. Settings keys need a prefix for every app, including the company's unprefixed seven.
- Personal records sit in the company's Sheet, next to the Apps Script's ledger. Separating them later means a migration.
- Every sync reads every app's tabs whole, since 12b keeps whole reads at sync.

### A middle shape

One storage for the company and one for everything personal: loans, expenses and later assets. That follows the legal line between a company and a person. Personal apps can then write together atomically, such as a loan payment and its budget entry, and the company Sheet stays clean. It costs one more Picker step or one app-created Sheet. This is my lean, and question 2 asks.

## Questions for the owner

1. **Name and address.** Does the hub keep the name Ledgerboard and the address `ipazanin.github.io/ledgerboard/`? Keeping the address keeps the installed app on your phone and everything this browser holds. A new address means installing again and choosing the Sheet again.
2. **Where personal data lives.** Three choices: (a) one Sheet per app, (b) everything in the company Sheet, (c) the company Sheet plus one personal Sheet for all personal apps. I lean to (c), because personal apps can then write together in one step and the company Sheet stays the company's. It costs one Picker step or one Sheet the app creates.
3. **Personal expenses and the company ledger engine.** Should personal expenses reuse the company app's bank import, categories, rules, review and transaction list with personal buckets, or be built separately? Reuse saves most of the work but makes the six company buckets configurable across 34 files. Separate is simpler now and duplicates the import and rules later.
4. **Your personal tax facts.** Residence city, birth year, second pillar and dependants sit in the company's settings today. Should they move to a person profile that every app reads, or stay with the company app and be published to the others?
5. **Currencies.** Will any app hold another currency, such as US stocks in the assets app or a loan in another currency? If not, the money format stays one per device and refactor 14 never happens.
6. **Path alias.** Moving folders rewrites about 1,500 relative imports. Do you want an alias such as `@shared/` introduced in the same move, so later moves stay small?
